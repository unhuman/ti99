| Bust-A-Bobble | `games/BustABobble/` | `BUSTABOB` + `BUSTAB2` | 🎮 Playable on TI-99 (verified in Classic99); CV builds, needs an emulator pass. **All 30 rounds, title screen, `8 3 8` round select.** Open: music, lives HUD, difficulty ramp (`DESIGN.md` §11b), pre-made groups (§7) | Clone of Taito's **Puzzle Bobble** / *Bust-A-Move* — the name is a play on both. CVBasic, **dual-target**: TI-99/4A (`BUSTABOB_8.bin`) and ColecoVision (`bustabob.rom`). **All 30 arcade rounds**, shapes transcribed cell-for-cell from the Puzzle Bobble FAQ's stage diagrams (`assets/arcade-stages.txt` → `transcribe_stages.py` → `levels.txt` → `levels.bas`); the source marks colours only where they matter strategically, so **shapes are authentic and colours are assigned**. The whole design hangs off one decision: bubbles are 2×2 chars and stay character-aligned in every state — 16-px pitch, 8-px (1-char) hex stagger, and an **8-px (1-char) ceiling descent**, so the field translates as a rigid body with no shifted pattern variants and no cell ever holding two bubbles. That makes the board shake, the ceiling drop, and the round-clear slide (remaining bubbles scrolling down past the bottom) all the *same* `SCREEN` blit at a different destination offset, from a 40-byte RAM row buffer that carries the **walls too**, so they shake with the field and bubbles can touch them; **the game stays playable through the shake** and the drop clock never pauses. The ceiling drops on a **timer**, not a shot count (the arcade uses shots that accelerate as colours are cleared — `DESIGN.md` §11). Matched groups **burst** through three dissolve frames and stranded orphans **fall away as sprites**. **Every bubble is ONE hue shaded by dithering** — two-tone balls need a *pair* of well-separated colours and this palette has no eight such pairs, which made red and "orange" the same ball in play; density is per colour and is what pulls white 2.2× clear of grey. Each round carries a **fixed 32-entry shot sequence** (a puzzle, not a slot machine) with deterministic substitution so a colour can never go dead. Score is a **BCD digit array in units of 10** with a fixed trailing `0` — the arcade's doubling drop bonus caps at 1,310,720 for one drop, past 16 bits before the score is touched. One moving object, 9 sprites, zero per-frame VDP reads, a 128-byte offline-generated aim table instead of trigonometry, no modulo anywhere. ~487 bytes RAM. `assets/genlevels.py` packs all 30 rounds into 1,860 B; `assets/prevlevels.py` renders screen mocks and **verifies the alignment invariant** over top×shake (0 violations); `assets/solvelevels.py` **proves all 30 rounds winnable** by re-implementing the game from the shipped `levels.bas`/`art.bas` (real flight, pixel collision, snap tie-breaks, substitution rule, drop clock) and finding a clearing line for each — a fixed shot sequence means a round *can* be impossible and nothing else would notice — and `--anchors` (wired into `build-ti.sh`) proves no round ships a bubble hanging from nothing, the malformed-data defect that produced three different apparent engine bugs before being fixed at source. HUD carries a 64-step drop-timer gauge and spare lives as little green creatures; the **death line is yellow and flashes red when crossed, drawn *through* the bubbles sitting on it** so the cue is not hidden by the very stack that triggered it. Open: transcribed vs. original layouts (`DESIGN.md` §7); which reading of the drop-scoring rule (`§11a`, one line). **A SECOND CART, `BUSTAB2` (Bust-A-Bobble 2), builds from the same source with `--expert`:** 50 generated levels and the arcade's real ceiling rule -- it drops on BUBBLES FIRED, every `b - missing colours` shots, so it accelerates as the board empties, and the HUD gauge becomes a magazine of shots remaining. Cart 1 is untouched and builds byte-identical. Levels are generated (`assets/genexpert.py`) rather than transcribed because Puzzle Bobble 2 is a two-player game and, more to the point, the arcade SHAPES were measured not to be what made it hard -- 28 of cart 1's 30 rounds clear before its timer fires once. Depth cannot supply the difficulty either: the death line caps usable layouts at 8 rows and the arcade set already reaches it. |
| Joust | `games/Joust/` | `JOUST` | 🛠 Phases 1-3 of 8 build on BOTH targets; not yet play-verified (needs an emulator pass) | Williams' 1982 **Joust** -- flap an ostrich, and whoever's lance is HIGHER at contact unhorses the other; the loser drops an egg, and an egg left alone hatches back one tier meaner. CVBasic, **dual-target**: TI-99/4A (`build-ti.sh` -> `JOUST_8.bin`) and ColecoVision (`build-coleco.sh` -> `joust.rom`). **Design written from arcade research BEFORE any code**, and that was not ceremony: writing it from memory first omitted the pterodactyl, the lava troll and the eroding floor outright, and got three numbers wrong. `DESIGN.md` §0 cites the original instruction card for every value and records where sources DISAGREE rather than smoothing it over -- Bounder 500 / Hunter 750 / Shadow Lord 1500, eggs 250-1000 with a **+500 mid-air catch bonus**, Survival 3000, extra bird every 20,000, Hunters from wave 4 and **Shadow Lords not until wave 16**. Physics is 8.8 fixed point with velocities **+32768 biased** so no comparison ever crosses zero (every `#var` compare is unsigned); horizontal wrap is free because 256 px x 256 overflows 16 bits exactly. Zero per-frame VDP reads -- the islands are a RAM table compared arithmetically, not a tile map -- and enemy AI is two comparisons per knight, no search, so the Ms. Pac-Man trap (actors x per-actor search) is absent by construction. Player is **sprite 0** because flicker is off and the VDP drops the highest-numbered sprites first. Three defects were caught by the compiler-safety checklist before the first build, including `do_death` being GOSUB'd but left by GOTO -- invisible on the TI, fatal on Coleco. Outstanding, phased in `DESIGN.md` §14: **arcade font**, **lava troll** (wave 3+, reach grows with the wave), **island erosion** (the bridge burns at wave 3, ledges from wave 6, Egg waves restore all), **pterodactyl** (wave 8 and any slow wave, killable only by a level head-on lance in the open mouth), and **Egg/Survival waves**. Gladiator and Team waves are two-player constructs and are recorded as deliberately omitted (§11). TI 10,892/24,336 with 13,444 free; Coleco 8 KB ROM, 230/814 RAM. |

| UFO! | `games/UFO/` | `UFO` | 🎮 All 8 phases build on BOTH targets; needs a runtime pass (loop-rate probe still on screen) | Ed Averett's 1981 **UFO!** for the Magnavox Odyssey² (*Satellite Attack* on Philips Videopac). CVBasic, **dual-target**: TI-99/4A (`build-ti.sh` -> `UFO_8.bin`) and ColecoVision (`build-coleco.sh` -> `ufo.rom`). The whole game is ONE VARIABLE: a 15-unit **force field** that is armour, ammunition and best weapon at once, and cannot be spent twice. ARMED only at full -- a shot costs a third, a ram empties it, and you move at HALF SPEED and are mortal for the three seconds it takes to come back. Contact is tested at TWO radii and which applies is the game: armed, the field kills at 14 px; unarmed, the bare hull dies at 9. **The gun only ever turns clockwise**, one 22.5° notch every 5 frames toward your heading and never the short way, so it is usually aimed where you were -- faithful to the one source that says which way it turns, and the reason ramming becomes the real tactic. Three enemies at the cartridge's own **1 / 3 / 10 points** (drifter, Hunter-Killer that LINKS a partner in when it acquires you, Light-speed Starship that bombs with guided missiles); every kill throws **three missiles** out, so chains cascade -- and at 1/3/10 a chain is legible, which a x100 scale would have destroyed. Satellites fake their spin by flipping between a PLUS and a CROSS, **the original's own trick** for rotating on hardware that could not. No terrain, so no actor ever asks the world where the ground is: zero per-frame VDP reads and x wraps free out of a 16-bit overflow. `DESIGN.md` §0 cites every mechanic to a source and records where they DISAGREE. New build gate `assets/checklayout.py` catches a `PRINT AT` that wraps past column 31 and a HUD `VPOKE` landing inside another routine's label -- its own first version compared only within a label and passed the very bug it existed to catch. TI 15,700/24,336; Coleco 16 KB ROM, 576/814 RAM. |
| Keystone Kapers | `games/KeystoneKapers/` | `KEYSTONE` | 📐 **Design complete — no code yet** (`DESIGN.md` §13 carries the 8-phase build plan) | Garry Kitchen's **Keystone Kapers** (Activision, Atari 2600, 1983). CVBasic, **dual-target**: TI-99/4A (`KEYSTONE_8.bin`) and ColecoVision (`keystone.rom`). Officer Keystone Kelly has **fifty seconds** to run Harry Hooligan down inside Southwick's Emporium before he reaches the roof — a store **eight screens wide and four levels tall**. The view **flips rather than scrolls**: eight discrete screens, one `SCREEN` blit per crossing, so the per-frame budget never pays for the store at all (RallyX's pan repaints 576 chars *every camera cell*; this repaints once every couple of seconds of running). That trade is the whole design — Harry can be one pixel off-screen and invisible, which promotes the **scanner** from overview to the primary instrument for finding him, so it is a Phase 6 requirement and not polish. **A screen is exactly 256 px, so an actor's x within its screen is one unsigned byte** — no 16-bit world coordinate, no unsigned-compare split, no scaling. **Zero per-frame VDP reads**: an actor's floor is an index into a four-entry height table, never a question asked of the screen, so the Ms. Pac-Man trap is absent by construction. Escalators stand at both ends but only one per floor goes **up**, alternating **west/east/west** — and since Kelly starts at the east entrance, reaching the roof on foot is **three full traverses of the store**, one per floor (getting the alternation backwards lands him on floor 1's escalator at spawn and silently shortens the store by a third). **Three shopping floors and a roof**; the centre **elevator is the only way down and the only shortcut**. Carts and cathedral radios cost **9 seconds** and are jumped, **toy biplanes cost a Kop** and are ducked, and the **beach ball is the only obstacle with two right answers** — its apex grows with the Krook, so a low one must be jumped and a high one **cannot be jumped at all** and must be **ducked** — the arc is capped 2 px below standing height so no ball is ever free to run under, which would have made the late-Krook balls the easiest ones. §5a picks apex, ducked height and hitbox inset *together* to leave a 3-px seam where both work: without it there is a ball height that can be neither jumped nor ducked, which would present as an occasional unfair hit and never as a reproducible bug, so it is verified by a sweep over every ball height rather than by playing. The 5-row floor band leaves exactly **16 px of headroom**, so ground hazards are drawn 8 px tall and the duck is a height change rather than a dodge. `DESIGN.md` §0 cites every mechanic and records four places the sources **disagree** (bonus bands 1-8/9-16/17+ vs 1-9/10-15/16+; three floors vs four; whether a biplane kills or costs 9 s; escalator direction). §8a flags the hazard unique to this game: it is the **first game here to read the joystick's vertical axis in anger**, and on the TI that axis shares a line with **ALPHA LOCK** — latched down it pins Kelly into a permanent duck, so the title screen detects it and refuses to start. |
