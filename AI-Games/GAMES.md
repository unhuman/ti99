| Bust-A-Bobble | `games/BustABobble/` | `BUSTABOB` | 🎮 Playable on TI-99 (verified in Classic99); CV builds, needs an emulator pass. **All 30 rounds, title screen, `8 3 8` round select.** Open: music, lives HUD, difficulty ramp (`DESIGN.md` §11b), pre-made groups (§7) | Clone of Taito's **Puzzle Bobble** / *Bust-A-Move* — the name is a play on both. CVBasic, **dual-target**: TI-99/4A (`BUSTABOB_8.bin`) and ColecoVision (`bustabob.rom`). **All 30 arcade rounds**, shapes transcribed cell-for-cell from the Puzzle Bobble FAQ's stage diagrams (`assets/arcade-stages.txt` → `transcribe_stages.py` → `levels.txt` → `levels.bas`); the source marks colours only where they matter strategically, so **shapes are authentic and colours are assigned**. The whole design hangs off one decision: bubbles are 2×2 chars and stay character-aligned in every state — 16-px pitch, 8-px (1-char) hex stagger, and an **8-px (1-char) ceiling descent**, so the field translates as a rigid body with no shifted pattern variants and no cell ever holding two bubbles. That makes the board shake, the ceiling drop, and the round-clear slide (remaining bubbles scrolling down past the bottom) all the *same* `SCREEN` blit at a different destination offset, from a 40-byte RAM row buffer that carries the **walls too**, so they shake with the field and bubbles can touch them; **the game stays playable through the shake** and the drop clock never pauses. The ceiling drops on a **timer**, not a shot count (the arcade uses shots that accelerate as colours are cleared — `DESIGN.md` §11). Matched groups **burst** through three dissolve frames and stranded orphans **fall away as sprites**. **Every bubble is ONE hue shaded by dithering** — two-tone balls need a *pair* of well-separated colours and this palette has no eight such pairs, which made red and "orange" the same ball in play; density is per colour and is what pulls white 2.2× clear of grey. Each round carries a **fixed 32-entry shot sequence** (a puzzle, not a slot machine) with deterministic substitution so a colour can never go dead. Score is a **BCD digit array in units of 10** with a fixed trailing `0` — the arcade's doubling drop bonus caps at 1,310,720 for one drop, past 16 bits before the score is touched. One moving object, 9 sprites, zero per-frame VDP reads, a 128-byte offline-generated aim table instead of trigonometry, no modulo anywhere. ~487 bytes RAM. `assets/genlevels.py` packs all 30 rounds into 1,860 B; `assets/prevlevels.py` renders screen mocks and **verifies the alignment invariant** over top×shake (0 violations); `assets/solvelevels.py` **proves all 30 rounds winnable** by re-implementing the game from the shipped `levels.bas`/`art.bas` (real flight, pixel collision, snap tie-breaks, substitution rule, drop clock) and finding a clearing line for each — a fixed shot sequence means a round *can* be impossible and nothing else would notice — and `--anchors` (wired into `build-ti.sh`) proves no round ships a bubble hanging from nothing, the malformed-data defect that produced three different apparent engine bugs before being fixed at source. HUD carries a 64-step drop-timer gauge and spare lives as little green creatures; the **death line is yellow and flashes red when crossed, drawn *through* the bubbles sitting on it** so the cue is not hidden by the very stack that triggered it. Open: transcribed vs. original layouts (`DESIGN.md` §7); which reading of the drop-scoring rule (`§11a`, one line). |
